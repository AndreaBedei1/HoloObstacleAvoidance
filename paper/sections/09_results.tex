\section{Results Available at This Stage}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the two blocks that are complete. Both were obtained
with \emph{simulator-oracle} obstacle perception rather than the visual
detector, and both are labelled accordingly throughout: they validate the
simulated plant and isolate the temporal estimator, and they are not visual
obstacle-avoidance results. The planner comparison (block C), the calibration
ladder (block D), and the physical campaign (block E) are not reported here.

\subsection{Simulation Integration Baseline}

The integration campaign accepted 9 of 9 runs across its three scenarios
under pre-registered criteria (Table~\ref{tab:baseline0}). Every avoidance
run produced the same state sequence with exactly one maneuver and no side
reversal, no collision occurred, minimum clearance to the obstacle surface
stayed in 0.672--0.715\,m, the vehicle returned to within 6\,cm of its
original line, and the transferable dead-reckoning error stayed at or below
0.20\,m. In the dropout scenario a deterministic 2\,s silence injected
mid-maneuver was survived in all three runs with no premature recovery, no
side switch, and no collision. Repeatability across fresh processes is tight
rather than exact --- 4\,cm spread in clearance, 0.3\,s in maneuver time ---
which is the expected consequence of distributed wall-clock scheduling.

Three failures from an earlier attempt are retained on record because two of
them are integration lessons that the physical stack will also face: a
middleware session that started isolated and produced a vehicle that never
moved, and a startup race in which the planner engaged before the navigation
estimator was publishing, producing a pose-less maneuver that diverged. Both
are now prevented by a graph-liveness gate and by gating perception on
navigation liveness. The third is a genuine weakness of the committed policy
under an early detection silence, and it is the motivating failure for the
temporal-estimation block rather than something the estimators were credited
with fixing after the fact.

\subsection{Temporal-Estimation Ablation}

The closed-loop ablation ran 100 runs with frozen parameters
(Table~\ref{tab:phase7b}). Two observations dominate. First, the methods are
\emph{indistinguishable on every continuous metric under nominal conditions}:
median clearance spans 0.708--0.713\,m, final lateral error 0.014--0.019\,m,
maneuver time 69.5--69.7\,s, and confirmation delay 1.01--1.03\,s across all
four variants. Second, all of the separation lives in the outlier scenario
E4, where the Kalman family scores 5/5 and 4/5 against 3/5 for both
non-probabilistic variants. The three grazing collisions in E4 occurred at
0.288--0.297\,m minimum clearance against a 0.30\,m threshold, with maneuvers
of normal shape but roughly 0.4\,m smaller lateral amplitude; all three fall
in the hold family and none in the Kalman family. Aggregate success is
therefore 25/25 for T2, 24/25 for T3, and 22/25 for both T0 and T1, and the
single T3 shortfall is an infrastructure freeze rather than a method
difference --- T3 is identical to T2 by construction here.

The prediction result runs in the opposite direction, and we report it as a
first-class finding rather than a caveat. During the dropout that begins at
maneuver onset, the simple hold predicts the obstacle's image-space position
substantially better than the constant-velocity filter: prediction-only
centre RMSE is 0.038 versus 0.084 in the early-dropout case and 0.036 versus
0.071 in the mid-maneuver case (Table~\ref{tab:onset},
Fig.~\ref{fig:onset}). The mechanism is measurable in the reference signal:
the obstacle's image-space centre velocity is zero before the vehicle
commits and $-0.032$\,units/s afterwards, so the constant-velocity model
extrapolates a velocity that the maneuver has just invalidated, while the
hold makes no velocity assumption at all. An exploratory damped
constant-velocity variant lands between the two (0.055 and 0.043) and does
not beat the hold; it was discarded as a benchmark method and is recorded
only as a direction for a maneuver-aware model.

Taken together, these two results say something more specific than ``the
Kalman filter helps''. The Kalman machinery earns its place through
$\chi^2$-gated outlier rejection, not through prediction accuracy: it is
outlier armour rather than a better predictor. That distinction matters for
the sim-to-real study, because outlier robustness and prediction accuracy are
stressed by different physical phenomena, and a simulator that reproduces one
faithfully need not reproduce the other.

\subsection{Noise Floor}

The residual failure rate is approximately one non-success per 25 runs from
single-run tail events, plus a roughly 2\% infrastructure mode in which the
vehicle remains pinned near its start. Both are counted as failures under the
pre-registered rules rather than reclassified. This floor is low enough to
compare planners on aggregate success and clearance, and it bounds what the
subsequent blocks can resolve: per-scenario differences smaller than about
two runs in five are not resolvable at this sample size, which is the reason
the planner campaign uses ten repetitions per cell.
