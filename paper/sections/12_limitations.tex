\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The scope of the physical evidence is narrow and we state it plainly. The
experiments take place in a controlled pool of roughly 8\,m length with
clear, shallow water, a V-shaped floor, and no controlled currents, against
static obstacles of known classes drawn from a small physical set; avoidance
is primarily horizontal at approximately constant depth. Nothing here
supports claims about open water, turbid water, currents, moving obstacles,
or unknown object classes.

The perception and navigation design carries its own constraints. Sensing is
camera-only, so obstacle range comes from a monocular known-size model whose
assumptions are violated when a bounding box is clipped by the image border;
the vehicle has no Doppler velocity log, so runtime navigation is
commanded-motion dead reckoning that drifts by design, and the drift figures
reported here are simulated ones. The external ground truth requires
refraction-aware calibration through the air--water interface before any of
its numbers may be used, and no accuracy figure for it is claimed yet.

Several components are deliberately incomplete. The adaptive-covariance
estimator T3 remains uncalibrated and is identical to T2 in every experiment
reported here, so no adaptive-estimation claim is made. The physical
vehicle's dynamics have not been identified, which means the \STHREE{} rung
of the ladder is specified but not measured, and the earlier rungs likewise
await their measurement campaigns. The planner comparison is running and its
outcome is not anticipated in this paper.

Finally, the results reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} use simulator-oracle
obstacle perception rather than the visual detector. They are therefore
evidence about the simulated plant, the navigation design, and the temporal
estimators, and they are not evidence about visual detection performance.
Reproducing the full visual pipeline requires assets and trained weights that
reside on a separate machine, and that dependency is recorded rather than
worked around.
