\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Simulation fidelity and sim-to-real evaluation}

HoloOcean provides the physics and rendering used in this
study~\cite{potokar2022holoocea,potokar2024holoocea}; OceanSim reflects
the broader effort to support underwater perception in
simulation~\cite{song2025oceansim}. Simulation quality, however, is not
equivalent to real-world predictivity. Kadian et al. formalise
sim-to-real predictivity, while Truong et al. show that higher fidelity
need not improve transfer~\cite{kadian2020simrealp,truong2022rethinki}.
Underwater work has examined direct high-fidelity transfer and
HoloOcean-based hardware-in-the-loop
evaluation~\cite{zhang2025simtoreal,meyers2025testinga}. We instead
freeze predictions before deployment and preserve the later diagnosis as
a separate evidence class.

\subsection{Vision-based underwater avoidance}

Attenuation, turbidity and uncertain monocular depth complicate underwater
vision. Relevant approaches include learned depth
estimation~\cite{yu2023udepthfa}, navigation combining monocular vision
with sonar and domain randomisation~\cite{yang2022monocula}, and direct vision-based
avoidance~\cite{drewsjr2016realtime,bergantin2024realtime,yao2023visionba}.
Our focus is not detector accuracy but the interval over which detector
acceptance gates produce any planner-usable observation.

\subsection{Scope of the contribution}

The dynamic-window approach is a standard local
baseline~\cite{fox1997thedynam}; an underwater adaptation is given by
Eriksen et al.~\cite{eriksen2016amodifie}. We compare it with a
committed-manoeuvre controller only in simulation because the physical
vehicle supplied no pose estimate for the dynamic-window planner. We
claim no new planner, detector or simulator. The contribution is an
experimentally traced deployment gap, including negative and post-hoc
evidence, rather than an algorithmic advance.
