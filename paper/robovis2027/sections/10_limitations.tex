\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We separate limitations of the physical campaign, defects we found in our
own frozen study, and limits of the calibration itself. The second group
is unusual to publish; we publish it because a frozen result whose flaws
are known privately is worth less than one whose flaws are stated.

\subsection{The physical campaign}

\emph{Eight runs, one planner, three geometries, one day.} The design
called for twenty runs across two planners; the dynamic-window planner
was never flown and the pool was drained after a single day. Two of the
three geometries have two runs each, and no claim rests on a physical
difference between geometries beyond the side-choice observation, whose
sample size is attached wherever it appears.

\emph{No external ground truth.} Nothing spatial about the physical runs
was measured. This most constrains the paper and also produces one of its
results: a camera-only field campaign of this kind can validate a stack's
command and perception domains and not its trajectory. Record external
tracking during the runs, not only before them.

\emph{Uncontrolled disturbance.} Pool recirculation was measured at
\SIrange{0.037}{0.053}{\metre\per\second}, of the same order as a third
of the vehicle's commanded surge, and was non-stationary. It is present
in every physical run and absent from every simulated one.

\emph{Orphaned processes contaminated part of the session.} Duplicate
planner instances survived process cleanup and published to the command
topic, which is why the admission criterion of
Section~\ref{sec:realresults} exists. Three admissible runs were recorded
at above-grid rates and are admitted on the measured-duplicate test; run
5 is excluded. We regard the criterion as defensible but note that it was
formulated after the data existed, which is exactly the circumstance in
which a threshold can be tuned to a desired answer. Ours is derived from
the recordings that had a single publisher by construction, and we state
it so a reader can disagree.

\subsection{Defects in the frozen study}

\emph{The observation relay and the planner disagree about the camera.}
The relay decides whether a detection survives by converting apparent
height to range at a \SI{60}{\degree} vertical field of view, while the
oracle projects and the planner inverts at \SI{90}{\degree}. The relay
therefore believes the obstacle roughly \num{1.5} times further away
than the planner does, and applies the measured detection-probability
curve to the wrong range band, in the sixty runs at S1 and above. The
frozen results are what they are and we do not restate them; the effect
is quantified as one rung of the post-deployment ablation.

\emph{The frozen runs are not reproducible}, for two independent
reasons. The relay's random seed is discarded when it is zero, the value
the campaign used, so the stochastic levels were seeded from system
entropy. And the campaign's command loop ran at about half its intended
rate -- \SI{10}{\hertz} on the nominal topic against \SI{20}{\hertz} on
an idle machine -- because that loop is wall-clock driven and twenty runs
ran back to back on a loaded machine, while the perception gate is
simulated-time driven and was unaffected.

\emph{A benchmark constant that nothing reads.} The frozen benchmark
records a \SI{60}{\degree} vertical field of view for the shared range
estimator. No node in either domain reads it; both used the planner's
\SI{90}{\degree} default. The domains therefore agree and no sim-real
mismatch follows, but a file whose purpose is to keep them aligned did
not enforce this value.

\emph{Individual run failures.} One frozen run of eighty ends in a
collision; two S2 runs never produced a planner-valid observation; four
dynamic-window runs have no commitment distance, making those cells
medians of four.

\subsection{The calibration}

The paired-observation dataset has no coverage between \SI{0.8} and
\SI{1.6}{\metre}, which is the band the physical runs spent most of their
time in. Command latency was never measured and is left at zero rather
than guessed. Rise-time constants have spreads as large as their values.
The translation axes are reported as symmetric because the sample cannot
distinguish their directions, not because symmetry was established, and
\texttt{sway+} rests on a single valid trial. Two actuator trials ended
in contact and are excluded from the fit with their reason recorded.

Most fundamentally, the ladder models the \emph{statistics} of the real
detector and not the detector. The frozen study never ran a visual
detector at all, and the property of the real detector that most shaped
the physical campaign -- the range window imposed by its acceptance
gates -- is not a statistic that any of S1, S2 or S3 could have
contained.

\subsection{What we would do differently}

Record external tracking during runs, not only before them. Derive the
scenario geometry from the basin rather than adapting the basin to a
scenario. Check that every parameter in a shared benchmark file is read
by something. Seed every stochastic component explicitly and assert the
seed at run time. And treat the acceptance gates of a perception module
as a first-class part of its interface, because in this study they
determined the shape of the experiment more than any quantity we
calibrated.
