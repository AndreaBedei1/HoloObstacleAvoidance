\begin{abstract}
We investigate why calibrating measured sensing and actuation did not yield
closed-loop transfer for camera-driven underwater obstacle avoidance. A HoloOcean
baseline was augmented with observation, timing and vehicle-response
models fitted to a BlueROV2. Eighty closed-loop simulations covering two
planners and two obstacle geometries were frozen, hashed and committed
before the physical campaign (\frozenmark). The deployed stack required
documented changes; nine runs were then acquired and eight were
admissible, all with the committed planner (\realmark). No overhead
tracking was recorded during these runs, so spatial metrics such as true
clearance and trajectory cannot be validated. The comparable command
stream nevertheless shows that all eight physical runs remained at the
planner's strafe setpoint, whereas all twenty corresponding frozen runs
reached its go-around setpoint. The principal mismatches were structural:
the detector accepted the obstacle only from \SI{0.29}{\metre} to
\SI{2.11}{\metre}; the vehicle started \SI{1.86}{\metre} away and
engaged at \SI{1.80}{\metre}; and a \SI{2.5}{\metre} clearing offset
could not fit the \SIrange{2.7}{3.0}{\metre}-wide basin. Eighty further
simulations performed only after deployment (\diagmark) isolate these
effects. Correcting a relay field-of-view defect improved simulated
clearance, but the tested pool-feasible manoeuvre rescaling was unsafe in
four of ten runs. Thus, calibrating measurable sensing and actuation
mismatches did not ensure transfer when deployment was governed by
structural mismatches absent from the model.
\keywords{Sim-to-real transfer \and Underwater obstacle avoidance.}
\end{abstract}
