A5 produces the largest single-factor increase in median clearance:
\SI{1.29}{\metre} to \SI{2.91}{\metre}, with zero rather than two
collisions. The relay had applied the measured detection-probability
curve in the wrong range band. This quantifies a defect in the frozen
simulator; it does not revise the frozen results.

A6 yields median clearance \SI{0.30}{\metre} and four collisions. The
tested pool-feasible rescaling was therefore unsafe. It indicates strong
sensitivity to detour width but does not establish that every
basin-compatible manoeuvre is unsafe.

A1--A3 individually improve median clearance relative to A0, and A3 has
no collisions. A4 is the only executed single-factor change that reduces
safety in this simulation, with three collisions. These post-hoc results
do not identify causal effects in the physical runs.

A0--A6 reach the go-around phase in all ten runs; A7 does so in six.
No physical run reached it. Because A7 includes the post-audit FOV fix
and the unflown A6 rescaling, it is a combined diagnostic configuration,
not the flown stack. More generally, the open simulated scene contains
no basin boundary, so none of these configurations reproduces the
structural constraint that dominated deployment.
