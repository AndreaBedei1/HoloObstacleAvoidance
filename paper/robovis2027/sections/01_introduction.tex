\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Underwater autonomy is commonly developed in simulation and judged in
water. The resulting deployment gap is often reported as a change in
success rate or error, without identifying its mechanism. We document
that mechanism for one camera-driven obstacle-avoidance stack, one
vehicle and one pool.

We tested whether measuring simulator mismatches improves closed-loop
transfer. Three models were fitted to the physical system: a static
observation model, a timing and burst-dropout model, and a vehicle model
covering saturation, deadband, asymmetry and lag. The vehicle model acts
downstream of the planner, so calibration never edits the controller.
Together with the baseline, these form levels S0--S3.

Before the vehicle entered the water, we executed, verified, hashed and
committed 80 closed-loop runs: four levels, two planners, two obstacle
geometries and five repetitions. These are predictive evidence
(\frozenmark), not reconstructions informed by the deployment.

The frozen stack did not act on the vehicle despite frequent detections.
Seven changes were made during the only available pool day. Nine runs
were then recorded with the committed planner and eight are admissible
(\realmark). The dynamic-window baseline could not be used because the
vehicle lacks the pose estimate it requires. The physical dataset is
closed.

Because the deployed and frozen stacks differ, the physical runs cannot
establish causal simulator predictivity or compare planners. They can
document what changed and motivate a post-deployment diagnosis.

Two structural mismatches dominate. Detector size gates create an
observable range window of \SIrange{0.29}{2.11}{\metre}. The vehicle
started \SI{1.86}{\metre} from the anchor and engaged at
\SI{1.80}{\metre}, eliminating the simulated approach phase. The planner
also requests a \SI{2.5}{\metre} lateral offset and a \SI{4}{\metre}
run-past manoeuvre in a basin only \SIrange{2.7}{3.0}{\metre} wide.
Neither mismatch was represented by S1--S3.

No external tracking was recorded during the runs. Trajectory, path
length, lateral excursion and true clearance therefore cannot be
recovered without assuming the plant behaviour being evaluated. We use
only the recorded command stream and the observation stream recovered
from the detector's annotated video. Spatial comparisons are excluded.

The final audit also found a field-of-view inconsistency in the
observation relay and a discarded random seed. We retain the frozen
results and report these defects explicitly.

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item a measurement-grounded S0--S3 calibration ladder, together with an
  80-run pre-deployment campaign with committed provenance;
\item a file-level account of the seven deployment changes and the
  structural mismatches that governed the real campaign;
\item an explicit command-domain comparison, with the boundary imposed
  by missing spatial ground truth; and
\item an 80-run post-deployment ablation that separates diagnostic
  evidence from the frozen predictions.
\end{enumerate}
