## Five closest papers

1. **Deep Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Underwater Navigation: A Comparative Study with DWA and Digital Twin Validation (Mari, Nawaf, Drap)** (2026)
   Occupies the exact headline combination a reviewer will pattern-match us to: real BlueROV2, obstacle avoidance, DWA as classical baseline, digital-twin simulation, sim-to-real transfer narrative. Its weaknesses (virtual obstacles only, no onboard perception, no estimator, no uncertainty, single fixed twin, no independent GT) define our differentiation line, but it means 'BlueROV2 avoidance vs DWA with sim-to-real' is no longer a novel frame by itself.

2. **Risk-Aware Belief Control Barrier Functions over Random Finite Sets (Han et al., TU Delft/KTH)** (2026)
   The strongest hardware-validated threat to the uncertainty-aware-avoidance claim: probabilistic multi-object belief (SMC-PHD filter) feeding risk-bounded collision avoidance on a REAL BlueROV2 with PHYSICAL obstacles in a tank, from top-tier groups. 'Belief-driven, risk-bounded avoidance on a real BlueROV2' is now occupied; our room is camera-only sensing, residual-calibrated (not assumed) noise with NIS/coverage, committed maneuver structure, and onboard (not mocap) state.

3. **DUViN: Diffusion-Based Underwater Visual Navigation via Knowledge-Transferred Depth Features** (2025)
   Closest experimental setting: camera-ONLY avoidance of deliberately placed physical obstacles on a real BlueROV2 in a controlled indoor water volume, with independent external optical ground truth (Qualisys mocap) and sim+real evaluation. It removes 'first camera-only, externally-ground-truthed monocular avoidance on BlueROV2' from our claimable set; what it lacks is everything interpretable/probabilistic (no estimator, no uncertainty, no calibration, no sim-calibration study).

4. **Real-time braking control based on optic flow divergence onboard an underwater vehicle (Bergantin, Viel, Jaulin)** (2024)
   Closest prior work to our estimator core: a monocular-vision-derived measurement fed to an EKF estimating relative vehicle-obstacle distance on a real ROV avoiding physical obstacles across visibility conditions. It kills any 'first camera-only probabilistic obstacle-distance estimation on a real ROV' claim; our estimator novelty must therefore rest entirely on quantitative residual-statistics calibration plus NIS/1-2-sigma consistency validation and closed-loop use, none of which they do.

5. **Sim2Real Predictivity: Does Evaluation in Simulation Predict Real-World Performance? (Kadian et al., RA-L)** (2020)
   The canonical formulation of our flagship question: does simulation predict real outcomes, is algorithm RANKING preserved (SRCC), and does tuning the simulator improve predictivity — with identical code running in sim and on the robot. Our S0-S3 study is structurally this experiment; our novelty is the underwater instantiation, the progressive MEASURED-calibration ladder (vs post-hoc parameter search), estimator+planner stacks (vs end-to-end RL), and estimator-consistency transfer. We must adopt SRCC explicitly or reviewers will ask why not.

### Evidence per verdict

**(1) Calibrated adaptive probabilistic visual obstacle estimator with statistically validated (NIS/1-2-sigma coverage) measurement-noise model** — *potentially_novel*

Every ingredient exists separately: adaptive/confidence-driven R is a mature MOT lineage (GIAOTracker's NSA-Kalman 2021, StrongSORT 2023, UTrack's NMS-variance R 2024, UncertaintyTrack's learned covariance with NLL/Energy-Score quality evaluation 2024, Deep LG-Track 2025); monocular-measurement-to-EKF obstacle-distance estimation on a real ROV exists (Bergantin 2024); confidence-adaptive tracking driving real-vehicle underwater avoidance exists on sonar (Li et al., AOR 2026); underwater adaptive-R filtering exists acoustically (2025 AIP paper) and for fish tracking (IMM-DeepSort 2025). NIS and sigma-coverage are standard Bar-Shalom consistency tools, not novel metrics. What no found paper does: QUANTITATIVE offline calibration of the measurement-noise model from residual statistics (sim oracle + real calibration trials) with filter-level NIS/coverage consistency validation, deployed closed-loop on a real robot. UncertaintyTrack is the nearest miss — it evaluates detector-output distributions with proper scoring rules but never checks filter consistency and has no robot.

**(2) Uncertainty-aware committed circumnavigation planner (chance-constraint / collision-probability corridor candidate)** — *too_weak*

Each pillar is occupied: the exact maneuver (vision-located obstacle -> committed semicircle bypass -> recovery to the original predefined line, multiple physical obstacles, real pool) is published (Zhang et al., JMSE 2022, deterministic stereo); rigorous joint chance-constrained collision avoidance validated on hardware is done (de Groot et al., IJRR 2025 Safe Horizon MPC); risk-bounded belief-based avoidance on a real BlueROV2 with physical obstacles is done (Han et al. 2026); covariance-inflated risk zones underwater (Li et al., JMSE 2024) and conformal-calibrated chance constraints + CVaR for AUV avoidance (Algorithms 2026) exist in simulation; uncertainty-modulated clearance dates to the 2017 velocity-obstacle underwater work. As a standalone 'new planner' claim this will not survive review.

**(3) Leveled simulation calibration study S0-S3 measuring predictiveness of real outcomes including algorithm-ranking preservation sim->real** — *clearly_novel*

No underwater analogue exists in the found literature. Kadian 2020 (ground, Habitat) established the question and SRCC but used post-hoc parameter tuning of a single sim, not a progressive measured-calibration ladder; Truong 2022 (CoRL) manipulated fidelity but for RL POLICY TRAINING, finding fidelity can hurt — a different regime from our evaluation-of-fixed-code transfer; Mahajan 2024 ranked sensor-noise-model fidelity by a downstream estimator but proprioceptive-only, open-loop, ground domain; Elmquist 2022 validated camera models by detector statistics, open-loop; Meyers 2025 (HoloOcean HIL) compares sim vs field descriptively at a single fidelity; Marinarium 2026 and LOTUSim 2026 provide infrastructure and anecdotal pool validation, no calibration-level-vs-predictiveness protocol; the 2025 simulator review explicitly names quantified sim-to-real transfer as an open gap. Component-wise measured calibration (camera, latency, dynamics, noise) + closed-loop avoidance outcomes + ranking preservation underwater is unoccupied.

**(4) Paired HoloOcean/physical-pool protocol with external overhead ground truth on physically existing obstacles** — *already_done*

Every element has direct precedent: DUViN 2025 runs camera-only BlueROV2 avoidance of physical obstacles in an indoor tank with independent Qualisys optical mocap ground truth; Alinei-Poiana 2024 is nearly our identical infrastructure (lab pool + overhead camera + BlueROV2 + onboard YOLO + EKF); Marinarium 2026 is verbatim an instrumented pool 'intermediate testbed between simulation and field deployment' with mocap and a paired digital twin including an underwater sim-to-real experiment; Meyers 2025 establishes the same-stack-in-HoloOcean-and-on-vehicle pattern; Winkel 2023 provides peer-reviewed through-surface external pose ground truth in a tank. The protocol per se is not a contribution.

**(5) The integrated system itself** — *too_weak*

Integrated camera-driven avoidance systems on real BlueROV2-class vehicles in pools are repeatedly published: Yang et al. ISRR 2022 (monocular+echosounder RL, pool, physical obstacles, sim-to-real), UIVNAV ICRA 2024 (camera-only BlueROV2, tank, physical obstacles), DUViN 2025, Ochoa 2022 (omnidirectional vision, pool), Zhang 2022 (stereo, pool), CORAL 2026 (BlueROV2 pool, sonar+VLM). 'We built and integrated it' carries no novelty weight at a good venue.


## Overall assessment

The work is publishable, but not with its current contribution ordering. Two of the five claimed contributions survive skeptical review: the S0-S3 calibration-ladder predictiveness/ranking study (clearly novel — no underwater, closed-loop, component-wise-measured-calibration analogue of Kadian 2020 exists, and simulator reviews explicitly name this gap) and the calibrated-with-consistency-validation adaptive estimator (potentially novel — the adaptive-R idea is a crowded MOT lineage and Bergantin already put monocular-vision-plus-EKF obstacle estimation on a real ROV, but nobody quantitatively calibrates R from residual statistics against an oracle AND validates filter consistency with NIS/coverage AND closes the loop on a robot). The planner contribution is too weak as claimed: the committed-circumnavigate-and-recover maneuver is published (Zhang 2022, real pool, physical obstacles), chance-constraint formulation is definitively done (de Groot IJRR 2025), and risk-bounded belief-based avoidance on a real BlueROV2 with physical obstacles is done (Han 2026). The paired-pool/twin protocol with external GT is already done in all its parts (DUViN, Marinarium, Alinei-Poiana, Meyers, Winkel), and 'the integrated system' is not a contribution at all given Yang 2022, UIVNAV 2024, and DUViN 2025. The single sentence a reviewer cannot find elsewhere: 'we measure whether progressively calibrating an underwater simulator with measured camera, latency, and vehicle-response models improves its ability to predict real closed-loop avoidance outcomes and to preserve the ranking of estimator/planner stacks, where those stacks include a residual-calibrated, NIS/coverage-validated perception-uncertainty pipeline.' Build the paper around that sentence. Main risks: (a) Truong 2022 provides the fidelity-doesn't-help counter-argument — mitigated because nothing is trained in sim, but it must be discussed and a null result per level pre-accepted as a finding; (b) if calibration yields no measurable estimator or closed-loop benefit over NSA-Kalman-style heuristics, contribution 1 evaporates — the ablations must be designed in from the start; (c) two threat papers (Li AOR 2026 sonar pipeline, Li JMSE 2024 covariance-inflated risk zones) are only aggregator-verified and must be read in full text before camera-ready claims are frozen.


## Recommended pivots

1) Reorder contributions: headline = the S0-S3 predictiveness/ranking study; second = calibrated-vs-heuristic adaptive estimator with NIS/coverage validation in closed loop; everything else becomes system description. 2) Adopt SRCC (Kadian 2020) verbatim as the ranking-preservation metric and add a per-level downstream predictiveness metric in the spirit of Mahajan 2024's VEPD; pre-register that a flat/negative calibration result at any level is reported as a finding, explicitly engaging Truong 2022. 3) Add NSA-Kalman ((1-c)R, GIAOTracker/StrongSORT) and a UTrack-style empirical-variance R as estimator ablation baselines — the paper's central empirical claim should be 'calibrated beats heuristic adaptive R, in coverage AND in closed-loop avoidance outcomes'; NCT 2023's Kalman-gain-chatter failure mode is ready-made motivation. 4) Reframe the planner as 'calibrated-uncertainty-modulated committed circumnavigation' — a behavior-level contribution, not a planner-class or constraint-math contribution; cite Zhang 2022 for the maneuver geometry, de Groot 2025 for the constraint family, Han 2026 for hardware risk-aware avoidance, and add the ablation calibrated-vs-uncalibrated covariance into the same planner. 5) Characterize the overhead RealSense ground truth as its own validated instrument (refraction model, error budget vs a known target) following Winkel 2023, and keep it strictly validation-only to differentiate from Alinei-Poiana. 6) Release code and data openly — none of the three closest competitors do, and it materially strengthens a systems-flavored submission. 7) Before freezing claims, obtain full texts of Li et al. AOR 2026 (confidence-adaptive sonar tracking + avoidance), Li et al. JMSE 2024 (covariance-inflated risk zone), and the Algorithms 2026 conformal-chance-constraint paper to confirm none quietly does residual-based calibration or consistency evaluation. 8) In Related Work, use the absence of any 2023-2026 dedicated underwater obstacle-avoidance survey and the 2025 simulator review's 'sim-to-real remains open' statement to frame the gap the headline contribution fills.


## Classical baseline recommendation

DWA, adapted to underwater vehicle dynamics, is the right classical baseline — and the literature actively supports it: Mari et al. 2026 already uses DWA as THE baseline for BlueROV2 avoidance (establishing it as the accepted comparator in exactly our setting), EROAS (IEEE JOE 2024) uses DWA and APF as its losing baselines, and the 2023-2025 marine literature (VADWA 2024, the 2025 path-planning review) shows DWA is the mainstream classical local planner underwater. Implement it following Eriksen et al. 2016 ('A modified dynamic window algorithm for horizontal collision avoidance for AUVs') — the canonical citation for why vanilla DWA must be modified for second-order underwater dynamics; citing and following it preempts the 'strawman DWA' objection. Two cautions: (1) Mari reported 76% collisions for DWA in their harbor setting — reviewers are primed to suspect under-tuned DWA baselines, so tune it honestly and report the tuning procedure; (2) feed DWA the SAME calibrated estimator's mean state (uncertainty discarded) rather than raw detections, so the comparison isolates uncertainty-USE rather than conflating it with estimation quality. Optionally add APF as a second cheap classical baseline (Ochoa 2022, EROAS precedent), but DWA is the mandatory one. As a bonus, a DWA-with-uncertainty-inflated-margins variant would connect to Dobrevski's adaptive-DWA line and strengthen the ranking-preservation algorithm set.
